Short pitch

A noir detective adventure in which the city has been stuck at 11:59 for forty nights, and the detective can borrow minutes from things that aren’t using theirs. Every borrowed minute goes in his ledger, and the villain reads the balance back to him in the final scene.

Longer description

Someone climbed the lighthouse at Lantern Bay and stole the Dawn. The police call it a scheduling issue. Otto Brass, a wind-up private detective three weeks behind on rent, takes the case because nobody else will.

What separates Brass & Midnight from other point-and-click adventures is its hint economy. Instead of a button that gives answers for free or for an ad, Otto borrows time from the stopped objects around him and spends it to make the city tell him what he is missing. The debt is recorded, can be repaid to the characters who need it most, and is read aloud by the antagonist at the end — a man who has himself been owed for forty years and has stopped extending credit. The player spends the game living the villain’s argument, then decides what to do about it.
