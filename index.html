Somebody stole the sunrise

Lantern Bay hasn’t seen a morning in forty nights.

Every clock reads 11:59. The tide is stuck halfway. Someone climbed the lighthouse and took the Dawn — the little piece of morning that rises over the city at six — and the police are calling it a scheduling issue.

Nobody hires a clockwork detective. Until tonight.

You are Otto Brass: wind-up private eye, one brass eye, three weeks behind on rent. A hand-drawn point-and-click adventure in four acts.

Borrow the time you need

The mechanic no other detective game has.

Borrow

Some things in this city aren’t using their minutes. A clock stopped at 11:59. A lighthouse with no lamp. A jukebox nobody feeds. Otto can take their time.

Spend

One minute buys a straight answer about whatever you’re stuck on. No ad, no purchase — just debt.

Repay

Give minutes back to the pelican who can’t wake up, the singer with no band, the clockmaker with no name. Or don’t.

Because in the last scene, the man who stopped the clocks asks Otto to turn out his pockets and reads the balance back to him. He has been owed for forty years. Now he wants to know what you owe.

“I was going to put them back.”
“So was I, Mr. Brass. For forty years I was going to put them back.”

Four acts, fourteen puzzles

  • The Lantern Docks — a combination lock, a newspaper crossword, a setlist riddle, and three dockhands of whom exactly one is lying.
  • The Jazz Quarter — a seized gear train, three clocks set by engraved rules, and a tape that only makes sense played backwards.
  • The Cliff Observatory — a constellation traced in a deduced order, and one beam of light bounced the length of a dark room.
  • The Harbour Clocktower — a minute that will not stop repeating, and an argument you have to win with the truth instead of a crowbar.

Puzzles start gentle and get properly hard. Nothing is a pixel hunt, nothing can be missed, and nothing can kill you. Three different endings.

Questions

How long is it?

Three to five hours for a careful player, across four acts. Act I alone runs about forty minutes and is free.

Can I get stuck?

No. Nothing is missable, there are no dead ends and no fail states. Hints nudge first, then push, then simply tell you the answer.

Does it need the internet?

No. The whole game runs on your device. The only thing that uses the network is a rewarded ad, if you choose to watch one.

Is there a PC or iOS version?

Not yet. Android first.
